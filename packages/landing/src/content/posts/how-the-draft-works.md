title: How the Draft Works
status: draft
---

The draft is the core of Challenger Fantasy, and it's worth understanding before you play your first game.
When a lobby fills, players take turns selecting fighters from the active card in a snake order — meaning the draft reverses direction each round. If you pick last in round one, you pick first in round two. This keeps things competitive and makes every pick position viable.
The goal is to build the highest-scoring roster by the time the card ends. Points come from real in-fight performance: finishes, strikes landed, takedowns, and more, with exact values depending on the game mode. You're not just hoping your fighters win — you're betting on how they'll fight.
A few things worth knowing going in:
Pick order matters more than it might seem. Early picks give you access to the highest-upside fighters, but the snake format means falling back to a late pick isn't a death sentence. Value exists throughout the draft if you know where to look.
Fighter style shapes your strategy. A high-volume striker might score consistently across a full three rounds. A fighter with finish upside might score less overall but spike hard if they get the stoppage. Building a balanced roster vs. swinging for ceiling is a real decision.
Game mode changes everything. Different modes weight scoring differently, which means a fighter who's a top pick in one format might be average in another. Paying attention to the format before you draft is half the battle.
The best way to get a feel for it is to play. Start a lobby, make your picks, and watch the card. It changes how you see every fight.
